# Guest post authorship read

Use this skill when you need a practical judgment about whether a guest post seems more likely written by a person or generated by AI.

## What this skill does
- Evaluates the text for signals of human authorship and AI generation.
- Returns a clear read, confidence level, and the strongest evidence for the judgment.
- Does **not** claim certainty; it gives an editorial assessment.

## What to look for
Examine the post for:
- **Specificity and lived experience**: concrete details, personal anecdotes, precise context, unusual but plausible observations.
- **Originality**: genuinely distinctive framing, fresh examples, or domain-specific insight.
- **Consistency**: stable tone, coherent argument, and consistent level of expertise.
- **Depth vs. surface polish**: whether it says a lot without actually saying much.
- **Repetition**: repeated ideas, rephrased sentences, looping conclusions, or redundant transitions.
- **Generic structure**: overly balanced lists, template-like intros/outros, and filler phrases.
- **Verbosity without commitment**: hedging, overexplaining, or avoiding concrete claims.
- **Local errors**: a human-like typo or idiosyncrasy can support human authorship, but is not proof.
- **AI-like patterns**: smooth but bland prose, broad claims, unnatural uniformity, frequent cliché, and section headings that feel mechanically organized.

## How to judge
1. Read the whole piece once for overall impression.
2. Scan for concrete evidence of lived experience or domain expertise.
3. Check whether the structure feels authored or template-driven.
4. Note any repeated phrasing, generic claims, or unnatural transitions.
5. Decide which side the balance favors:
   - **Likely human-written**
   - **Likely AI-generated**
   - **Unclear / mixed signals**

## Output format
Provide:
- **Verdict**: one of the three categories above.
- **Confidence**: low, medium, or high.
- **Why**: 3–6 bullets with the strongest evidence.
- **Notes**: mention any important caveat, such as missing context, heavy editing, or the possibility of a human using AI assistance.

## Guidance
- Do not pretend this is definitive detection.
- Do not focus on style alone; weigh content specificity and authorial voice more heavily.
- If the text is short, heavily edited, translated, or highly formal, say the evidence is limited.
- If signals conflict, choose **Unclear / mixed signals** rather than forcing a verdict.
