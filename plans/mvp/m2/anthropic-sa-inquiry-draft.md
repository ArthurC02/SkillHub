## 1. 正文

**Subject:** Licensing question — indexing `anthropics/skills` Skills in a third-party catalogue

Hello,

I am building [PROJECT NAME], a [one-line description: a search engine and testing sandbox for Agent Skills], and four Skills from `anthropics/skills` — `docx`, `pdf`, `pptx` and `xlsx` — are among the ones our catalogue lists. Each of those directories carries a `LICENSE.txt` stating that the materials are © Anthropic, PBC, all rights reserved, and that use is governed by the Consumer or Commercial Terms, with distribution and with "extracting, copying, creating derivative works, or reproducing the materials outside the Services" both prohibited.

We would like to be sure we are inside those terms rather than assume it, so I am writing to describe exactly what our platform does with the content and to ask whether it is permitted.

**What we do today, in four separate behaviours:**

1. **Indexing.** We fetch the Skill directory from the public repository at a pinned commit, store a copy of its files, record a content hash, and build a keyword and semantic search index over it. The stored copy is internal: it is what search runs against and what the sandbox unpacks, and it is not offered to users as a file.
2. **Description.** We show a plain-language summary, the Skill's stated limitations, its declared dependencies, a link to your repository, and the licence as we found it. The summary is generated by a language model from the Skill's own document and is labelled in the UI as machine-generated. It restates; it is not intended to substitute for the original.
3. **Sandboxed execution.** A signed-in user can run a Skill against their own test data inside an isolated sandbox on our infrastructure. The Skill's files are unpacked into that sandbox for the duration of the run, and the outputs go only to that user's private workspace.
4. **No download.** We do not offer these four Skills for download or export in any form, and our packaging pipeline blocks them explicitly. Users can run them on our platform; they cannot obtain a copy from us.

**What we have already changed while waiting for an answer.** As of [DATE], we have turned off two of these behaviours for these four Skills specifically, because they are the ones that most resemble reproduction: we no longer display the full text of `SKILL.md` or the package file tree, and we no longer allow the Skills to be run in our sandbox. They remain listed and searchable, with the summary, the licence and a link to your repository. We would rather over-restrict pending your answer than assume permission.

**Our questions:**

1. Does storing a copy of these Skill files in order to index and search them fall within "reproducing the materials outside the Services", or is a third-party catalogue's index of a public repository outside what that clause is aimed at?
2. If indexing is permitted, are machine-generated summaries and vector embeddings derived from the materials also permitted, given that the same clause names derivative works?
3. Would displaying the `SKILL.md` text in full — with attribution, the licence notice, and a link to your repository — be acceptable, or should that stay off?
4. Would running these Skills in a sandbox on behalf of an individual user, with no copy handed to them, be acceptable?

**What we commit to, regardless of the answer:**

- We keep the licence notice and attribution with every listing, and we link back to your repository as the source.
- We do not offer these Skills for download, and we will not without explicit written permission.
- We do not present them as our own work, and we do not remove or alter your `LICENSE.txt`.
- If the answer to any question above is no, we will remove the corresponding behaviour promptly, up to and including delisting the four Skills entirely.

If a written clarification is not something you can give, a pointer to the right team or to existing guidance would help just as much.

Thank you for the time,
